## 1. Fiction workflow

Idea → premise → characters → outline → scene list → draft → revise → polish. For a long project: write one chapter at a time, summarize each in 3 lines, update the continuity file, then continue. Ask the user only for choices that change the whole story (genre, POV, tone, ending direction); otherwise decide and note it.

## 2. Story foundation

- **Premise line:** `When [inciting event], a [protagonist with flaw] must [goal] or else [stakes].`
- **Protagonist:** want (external goal), need (internal lesson), flaw, fear, secret.
- **Antagonist force:** person, society, nature, or self, with a coherent reason and real power.
- **Stakes:** what is lost; make them personal, then escalate.
- **Theme:** the question the story argues (for example "Is loyalty worth the truth?"). Let it emerge through choices, not speeches.
- **Ending:** decide it early (win, loss, bittersweet, ambiguous); make the middle earn it.

## 3. Structure templates

**Three acts:** Act 1 (25%): world, want, inciting incident, first turning point. Act 2 (50%): rising complications, midpoint reversal (new information changes the goal), all-is-lost. Act 3 (25%): climax, resolution.

**Beat sheet (novel, about 15 beats):** opening image · setup · theme stated · catalyst · debate · break into two · B-story · fun and games · midpoint · bad guys close in · all is lost · dark night · break into three · finale · closing image.

**Hero's journey (12 stages):** ordinary world · call · refusal · mentor · threshold · tests/allies/enemies · approach · ordeal · reward · road back · resurrection · return.

**Mystery:** crime/question → suspects with motive, means, and secrets → clues planted fairly → red herrings → reversal → reveal that re-reads the clues.

**Romance:** meet → spark → obstacle (internal and external) → deepening → crisis → grand gesture/choice → union.

**Word-count guide:** short story 1-7.5k · novella 20-40k · novel 70-100k (varies by genre) · chapter 2-4k.

## 4. Scene craft

Every scene has: **goal** (what the POV character wants now), **conflict** (what stops them), **turn** (something changes: information, power, or emotion), **exit hook**. If nothing changes, cut or merge. Enter late, leave early. Ground each scene in place, time, and a sensory anchor within the first lines.

Scene card template:
```
Scene #: __  POV: __  Place/Time: __
Goal: __   Conflict: __   Turn: __
Emotional shift: __ → __
New information: __   Setup/payoff: __
Hook out: __
```
Pacing: alternate action and reaction (sequel: emotion, thought, decision). Raise stakes every 2-3 scenes. Vary scene length and intensity.

## 5. Character

Sheet: name, age, role, look (2 telling details), voice (rhythm, slang, verbal tic), want, need, flaw, fear, secret, relationships, arc (start belief → end belief). Make choices reveal character under pressure. Contradictions make people real (brave but petty). Give side characters one vivid trait and their own goal. Arc test: could you swap the protagonist's choice with anyone else's? If so, sharpen.

## 6. Dialogue

Subtext: characters rarely say exactly what they mean; they deflect, joke, or ask the wrong question. Each speaker sounds different (sentence length, vocabulary, interruptions). Cut greetings and small talk. Use action beats instead of adverb tags; `said` is invisible. No info-dump ("As you know, Bob"). Let conflict live in dialogue: each person wants something from the other.

Flat:
> "I'm angry that you lied to me," she said angrily.

Better:
> She set the two cups down and did not sit. "Funny. Maya says you were at the lake."
> "Maya talks."
> "So do you. Apparently to everyone but me."

## 7. Voice, POV, and style

POV choices: first person (intimate, limited), close third (flexible, most common), omniscient (wide, needs control), second (rare, intense). Keep one POV per scene. Tense: past (default) or present (immediacy); do not drift. Narrative distance: how deep in the character's head; keep it consistent. Show emotion through body, action, and perception; use a direct statement of emotion when speed matters. Vary sentence length; choose specific nouns; keep metaphors fresh and few; cut adverbs that prop up weak verbs. Establish a voice sample first (a paragraph) and match it throughout.

## 8. Worldbuilding

Only build what the story touches. Sheet: geography and climate → people and cultures → power (who rules, how, who resists) → economy (what people eat, trade, fear) → history (three events that still shape the present) → magic/technology rules → limits and costs (a system without cost is a plot hole) → daily life details. Reveal through character experience, not lectures. Consistency beats size. Names: a naming language per culture (sound patterns), avoid unpronounceable strings.

Magic/tech system test: what can it do, what can it not do, what does it cost, who has it, and how does the plot break if the hero used it cleverly? Answer these before writing.

## 9. Genre notes

Fantasy (rules, cost of magic, cultures, quest structure) · Sci-fi (one big "what if", consequences worked out, tech as pressure on people) · Thriller (ticking clock, reversals, short chapters) · Horror (dread over gore, isolation, violated rules, the wrong detail) · Romance (chemistry through banter and vulnerability, satisfying ending guaranteed) · Mystery (fair clues) · Literary (interiority, language, ambiguity) · YA (immediacy, voice, first times, stakes that feel huge) · Comedy (specificity, escalation, timing, straight-faced characters) · Slice-of-life (small stakes made vivid) · Game/interactive fiction (branching state variables, choices with consequences, avoid illusion of choice).

## 10. Continuity system for long works

Maintain a `story-bible` (in the conversation or a file) with: characters (traits, ages, voice, status), timeline (dated events), locations, rules of the world, objects and where they are, open threads and promises to the reader, and a style sheet (POV, tense, spelling, names, terms). After each chapter add a 3-line summary and update open threads. Before writing a chapter, restate the anchor: chapter goal, what the reader already knows, what changes. Series: keep a running "what each character knows" table to avoid knowledge leaks.

## 11. Editing passes for fiction

1. **Structure**: is the arc clear, do the acts turn, does every scene change something?
2. **Character**: do choices come from want/flaw; is the arc earned?
3. **Continuity**: names, dates, injuries, objects, who knows what.
4. **Pacing**: cut the slow middles; end chapters on a hook or emotional beat.
5. **Line edit**: cut filler, sharpen verbs, vary sentence starts, remove repetition and pet phrases.
6. **Read aloud** for rhythm and dialogue.

## 12. Nonfiction books

**Foundation:** reader, transformation promise (`After this book you can ...`), unique angle, one-sentence pitch, comparable titles.
**Book architecture:** hook/introduction (problem + promise + how to use the book) → parts → chapters → conclusion (synthesis + next step). Each chapter: hook (story or claim) → problem → framework/idea → evidence and examples → practice/exercises → key takeaways → bridge to the next chapter.
**Types:** how-to (steps, exercises), narrative nonfiction (scene-based, research woven in), memoir (theme first, selective truth, reflection on the past self), textbook (objectives, glossary, practice, summaries), business book (framework named memorably, cases, tools).
**Process:** chapter map with word targets → research notes → draft chapter by chapter → 3-line summaries → consistency pass on terms → cut repetition. Keep a style sheet (terms, tone, tense, person). Facts and quotes need sources; never invent quotes or statistics.
**Front/back matter:** title, subtitle, blurb (problem → promise → proof), table of contents, acknowledgments, resources.

## 13. Short forms

- **Flash fiction (under 1000 words):** one moment, one turn, ending that reframes the opening.
- **Poetry (original only):** choose a form (free verse, haiku 5-7-5, sonnet 14 lines ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, villanelle, ghazal), concrete images, line breaks that carry meaning, cut the last line if it explains. Do not reproduce existing poems or lyrics.
- **Song lyrics (original only):** hook first, verse-chorus-bridge, consistent syllable counts, rhyme with purpose (perfect and slant), one clear emotion.
- **Screenplay:** slug line, action lines in present tense, dialogue centered, one page is about one minute.
- **Comics/manga script:** page and panel breakdown with visual description, dialogue, and captions.
- **Game narrative:** premise, cast, quest lines, branching chart, barks (short repeated lines) per situation.

## 14. Verification

Confirm premise, POV, tense, and voice are consistent; every scene changes something; setups have payoffs; timeline and names are consistent with the story bible; the ending resolves the promise made in the opening; no reproduced copyrighted text; the requested length and format are met.
